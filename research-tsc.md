# Threshold Sound Conditioning (TSC) — Research Summary

A reference document for the scientific basis of the Tinnitus Helper app.
Covers the mechanism, key clinical studies, preclinical evidence, and protocol details extracted from the literature.

---

## What is Threshold Sound Conditioning?

Threshold Sound Conditioning (TSC) is the delivery of acoustic stimuli at or just below an individual's hearing threshold at specific frequencies. Unlike masking (which covers up tinnitus) or broadband noise therapies, TSC targets the exact frequencies where hearing loss exists, with the goal of stimulating residual hair cell and neural activity to drive neuroplastic recovery.

The core principle: the auditory system exhibits **use-dependent plasticity**. Gentle, calibrated sub-threshold stimulation can upregulate cochlear sensitivity, prevent further degradation, and in some cases partially restore lost function.

## Clinical Studies (Human)

### 1. Randomized Controlled Trial — Stanford University School of Medicine (2015)

> Cho S, Kwak E, Kwak S, Lopez J.
> **"Randomized Controlled Trial Evaluating Threshold Sound Conditioning in the Treatment of Sensorineural Hearing Loss (S26.006)"**
> *Neurology.* 2015;84(14 Supplement).
> DOI: [10.1212/WNL.84.14_supplement.S26.006](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.84.14_supplement.S26.006)

- **Design:** Double-blind RCT
- **Institution:** Stanford University School of Medicine
- **Objective:** Evaluate safety and efficacy of TSC as a treatment for SNHL
- **Method:** Subjects received tones played at their individual frequency-specific thresholds (determined by audiogram); active vs. sham conditions
- **Outcome:** Demonstrated statistically significant threshold improvements in the TSC group vs. control
- **Significance:** First RCT of TSC in humans; the foundational clinical validation for the mechanism

> **Protocol notes (to extract from paper):** Stimulation frequencies matched to individual audiogram; tones delivered at threshold level. Specific Hz values and tremolo parameters not yet confirmed — _scrape paper for exact values_.

### 2. Safety and Efficacy Study — Kwak & Kwak (2020)

> Kwak E, Kwak S.
> **"Threshold sound conditioning in the treatment of sensorineural hearing loss"**
> *Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology.* 2020;5(4):580–588.
> DOI: [10.1002/lio2.399](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/lio2.399)
> PMID: 32913738 | PMC: PMC7314479

- **Design:** Prospective clinical study
- **Objective:** Evaluate safety and efficacy of TSC for SNHL
- **Key finding:** TSC was safe and produced measurable hearing threshold improvements in participants with mild-to-moderate SNHL
- **Tinnitus relevance:** Authors note tinnitus is a common comorbidity of SNHL; threshold improvement at tinnitus-matching frequencies correlates with tinnitus relief
- **Significance:** Full peer-reviewed paper expanding on the 2015 Stanford abstract; forms the primary clinical evidence base

> **Protocol notes:** Frequencies targeted individually per audiogram. Standard TSC protocol used. _Scrape for exact stimulus levels, session duration, treatment duration_.

## Preclinical Studies (Animal Models)

### Restoration / Enhancement of Cochlear Sensitivity

#### 3. Long-Term Sound Conditioning Enhances Cochlear Sensitivity — Kujawa & Liberman (1999)

> Kujawa SG, Liberman MC.
> **"Long-term sound conditioning enhances cochlear sensitivity"**
> *Journal of Neurophysiology.* 1999;82(2):863–873.
> PMID: [10444683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10444683/)

- **Model:** Guinea pig
- **Stimulus:** Broadband conditioning sound at moderate levels
- **Finding:** Long-term exposure to sub-damaging sound conditioning enhanced distortion-product otoacoustic emissions (DPOAEs) and ABR thresholds — i.e., cochlear sensitivity *improved*
- **Mechanism:** Upregulation of the medial olivocochlear (MOC) efferent system; enhanced outer hair cell motility
- **Significance:** Seminal preclinical paper showing that sub-threshold stimulation can actively improve cochlear sensitivity — not just prevent loss

#### 4. Acoustic Deprivation Impairs Hair Cell Recovery — Fukushima, White & Harrison (1990)

> Fukushima N, White P, Harrison RV.
> **"Influence of acoustic deprivation on recovery of hair cells after acoustic trauma"**
> *Hearing Research.* 1990;50(1–2):107–118.
> PMID: [2076966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2076966/)

- **Model:** Animal model post-acoustic trauma
- **Finding:** Acoustic deprivation *after* trauma impaired hair cell recovery; sound stimulation promoted recovery
- **Significance:** Establishes the inverse relationship — silence is harmful; the auditory system needs input to recover

### Prevention of Noise-Induced Hearing Loss

#### 5. Protection by Low-Level Pre-Exposure — Canlon, Borg & Flock (1988)

> Canlon B, Borg E, Flock A.
> **"Protection against noise trauma by pre-exposure to a low level acoustic stimulus"**
> *Hearing Research.* 1988;34(2):197–200.
> PMID: [3170362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3170362/)

- **Model:** Guinea pig
- **Protocol:** 1 kHz conditioning tone at 81 dB SPL for 10 min/day × 10 days, then traumatic noise (0.5–8 kHz, 106 dB SPL, 2h)
- **Finding:** Pre-conditioned animals showed dramatically reduced permanent threshold shifts vs. controls
- **Significance:** First paper demonstrating acoustic conditioning protection; established the field

> **Protocol notes:** **Conditioning tone: 1 kHz, 81 dB SPL.** Traumatic noise: broadband 0.5–8 kHz at 106 dB SPL. This paper contains specific Hz and level data — useful for calibrating the app's approach.

#### 6. Forward and Backward Sound Conditioning — Niu, Tahera & Canlon (2004)

> Niu X, Tahera Y, Canlon B.
> **"Protection against acoustic trauma by forward and backward sound conditioning"**
> *Audiology & Neurotology.* 2004;9(5):265–273.
> PMID: [15316199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15316199/)

- **Finding:** Protection occurs whether conditioning precedes or follows the traumatic exposure ("forward" and "backward" conditioning)
- **Clinical relevance:** Relevant for both prevention (in at-risk users) and treatment (already-established hearing loss)

#### 7. Enriched Acoustic Environment After Trauma — Noreña & Eggermont (2005)

> Noreña AJ, Eggermont JJ.
> **"Enriched acoustic environment after noise trauma reduces hearing loss and prevents cortical map reorganization"**
> *Journal of Neuroscience.* 2005;25(3):699–705.
> PMID: [15659607](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15659607/)

- **Model:** Cat
- **Protocol:** Post-trauma exposure to an enriched acoustic environment containing the frequencies in the hearing loss range
- **Finding:** Reduced permanent threshold shifts AND prevented maladaptive cortical reorganization (the neural correlate of tinnitus)
- **Significance:** Directly links targeted frequency stimulation to prevention of tinnitus-generating cortical changes

> **Protocol notes:** Frequencies used matched the traumatized frequency region. _Scrape paper for exact Hz values and stimulus levels._

#### 8 & 9. Progressive Resistance to Noise — Campo, Subramaniam & Henderson (1991)

> Subramaniam M, Campo P, Henderson D.
> **"The effect of exposure level on the development of progressive resistance to noise"**
> *Hearing Research.* 1991;52(1):181–187.
> PMID: [2061206](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2061206/)

> Campo P, Subramaniam M, Henderson D.
> **"The effect of 'conditioning' exposures on hearing loss from traumatic exposure"**
> *Hearing Research.* 1991;55(2):195–200.
> PMID: [1757287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1757287/)

- **Finding:** Low-level conditioning exposures confer dose-dependent resistance to subsequent noise-induced hearing loss
- **Significance:** Demonstrates a protective threshold effect — sub-damaging sound makes the cochlea more resilient

### Augmented Acoustic Environment & Age-Related Hearing Loss

#### 10–12. Willott & Turner Series (1998–2000)

> Turner JG, Willott JF. "Exposure to an augmented acoustic environment alters auditory function in hearing-impaired DBA/2J mice." *Hearing Research.* 1998;118(1–2):101–113. PMID: [9606065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9606065/)

> Willott JF, Turner JG. "Prolonged exposure to an augmented acoustic environment ameliorates age-related auditory changes in C57BL/6J and DBA/2J mice." *Hearing Research.* 1999;135(1–2):78–88. PMID: [10491957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10491957/)

> Willott JF, Turner JG, Sundin VS. "Effects of exposure to an augmented acoustic environment on auditory function in mice: roles of hearing loss and age during treatment." *Hearing Research.* 2000;142(1–2):79–88. PMID: [10748331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10748331/)

- **Model:** Mice with progressive age-related hearing loss (C57BL/6J and DBA/2J strains)
- **Protocol:** Prolonged exposure to broadband augmented acoustic environment (non-damaging levels) from young adulthood
- **Finding:** Treated mice showed significantly better hearing thresholds compared to unexposed controls of the same age; effect was largest when treatment started before substantial loss had occurred
- **Significance:** Evidence that TSC-type stimulation slows age-related hearing decline; supports use for presbycusis as well as NIHL

## Proposed Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Key Paper(s) | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| Medial olivocochlear (MOC) efferent activation | Kujawa & Liberman 1999; Brown et al. 1998 | Sub-threshold stimulation engages the MOC reflex, enhancing outer hair cell stiffness and protecting/restoring sensitivity |
| Antioxidant upregulation | Jacono et al. 1998 | Conditioning increases cochlear antioxidant enzymes (SOD, catalase), reducing oxidative damage from noise |
| Prevention of cortical reorganization | Noreña & Eggermont 2005 | Targeted sound stimulation prevents the maladaptive cortical map changes that generate phantom sound (tinnitus) |
| Use-dependent hair cell plasticity | Fukushima et al. 1990; Canlon et al. 1988 | Cochlear hair cells respond to acoustic input; deprivation worsens damage, stimulation promotes recovery |

## Tremolo / Amplitude Modulation — Open Question

The app currently uses 7 Hz amplitude modulation (tremolo) on each tone. This is not directly studied in the TSC literature as found so far. However, there is related evidence:

- **MOC activation** is known to be enhanced by temporally varying sounds vs. pure steady tones
- **AM modulation** may more effectively engage the olivocochlear efferent pathway, potentially increasing conditioning efficacy
- **Recommended action:** Search specifically for AM modulation + olivocochlear + cochlear conditioning to find supporting literature, or contact the Stanford group

## Competitor Research Basis (AudioCardio)

AudioCardio (Stanford spin-out) is the primary commercial application of TSC. Their research page lists the same body of literature and cites the Cho et al. 2015 Stanford RCT as their foundational study. The Kwak & Kwak 2020 paper in *Laryngoscope Investigative Otolaryngology* is the full peer-reviewed publication of that work.

Our app targets the same mechanism but adds:
- Multi-band simultaneous stimulation (vs. AudioCardio's sequential/single-band approach — confirm by testing their app)
- Tinnitus-specific calibration workflow
- Open protocol (users can see and adjust exact levels)
